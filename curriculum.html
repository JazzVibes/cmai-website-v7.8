Glossary

Plain-language reference for class, rank material, lineage notes, and historical terms.

Dojo

The training hall. Students treat the space, instructors, and partners with care because the room is built around shared practice.

Rei

A bow, and also the habit of respect behind it. Students bow entering class, leaving class, and working with partners.

Osu

A short respectful acknowledgement. In class it usually means understood, yes, or ready.

Gi / Obi

The training uniform and belt. The belt marks rank, but the uniform also reminds everyone to train safely and consistently.

Kyu / Dan

Kyu ranks are student levels before black belt. Dan ranks are black-belt levels that continue the learning path.

Sensei / Guro

Sensei is a Japanese term for teacher. Guro is used in Filipino martial arts for an instructor or teacher.

Renshi / Shihan Dai

Renshi is an advanced teaching title. Shihan Dai means assistant or candidate-level teacher under a senior instructor's direction.

Kihon

Basics: stances, footwork, blocks, strikes, posture, and movement habits that make later material work.

Kata

A structured form. Kata preserves movement patterns, body mechanics, timing, and self-defense ideas for repeated study.

Bunkai

Application study. Bunkai asks what a kata movement can do with a partner: strike, turn, lock, throw, escape, or control.

Kumite

Partner exchange. Depending on level, it may mean prearranged drills, controlled sparring, or timing work with a partner.

Tuite

Okinawan joint-control and manipulation skills. Tuite uses structure, angle, and balance instead of relying on strength.

Kyusho

Vital-point and targeting concepts. These ideas are taught with control, safety rules, and practical body mechanics.

Makiwara

A traditional striking post used to study alignment, conditioning, and how a strike connects to the floor through the body.

Pinan / Heian

Related kata names used in different karate lines. These forms organize beginner-to-intermediate movement and application ideas.

Naihanchi / Seisan

Traditional kata names often used to build posture, close-range mechanics, turning power, and stable footwork.

Ryukyu Kempo

Okinawan karate language connected to the Ryukyu Islands. It emphasizes practical self-defense, kata application, and body mechanics.

Toudi / Te

Older Okinawan terms often translated as Chinese hand or hand. They point to karate's roots before the modern name karate became standard.

Shuri-te / Naha-te / Tomari-te

Older regional labels associated with Okinawan training around Shuri, Naha, and Tomari. Many later karate systems grew from these streams.

Karate ryu

Ryu means style or school. Names like Goju-ryu, Shorin-ryu, Shito-ryu, and Shotokan identify different karate lineages.

Small Circle Jujitsu

Professor Wally Jay's joint-control system. It focuses on small-radius movement, two-way pressure, balance breaking, and efficient control.

Jujitsu / Jujutsu

Two common romanizations for related Japanese grappling and control arts. Different schools, books, and lineages often prefer different spellings.

Danzan Ryu / Kodenkan

Seishiro Okazaki's Hawaiian jujutsu tradition and school name. It is an important background stream for Wally Jay's Small Circle work.

Judo / Kodokan

Judo is Jigoro Kano's modern grappling art. Kodokan is the school and institution Kano founded to organize and transmit it.

Kito-ryu / Koshiki-no-kata

Kito-ryu is an older jujutsu school studied by Kano. Koshiki-no-kata preserves classical principles inside judo practice.

Menjo

A certificate or license document. Historical menjo help trace who studied with whom and when a teacher received recognition.

Modern Arnis

Grandmaster Remy Presas's Filipino martial arts system, using stick work, empty-hand translations, disarms, flow, and practical movement.

Arnis

A Filipino martial art associated with stick, blade, and empty-hand methods. Modern Arnis is one major modern teaching system within that world.

Tapi-Tapi

A Modern Arnis flow drill for timing, sensitivity, entries, checking hands, disarms, and empty-hand transfer.

Open mat

Supervised practice time outside the normal class plan. Students review material, ask questions, and work at an appropriate pace.

Seminar / Testing

A seminar is a focused training event, often with a visiting instructor. Testing is a formal review of rank material when students are ready.

Rank overview

Full checklists are available in class. This overview helps guide practice between classes.

White to Yellow

  • Etiquette and safety
  • Stances, footwork, blocks, strikes
  • Breakfall introduction
  • Wrist releases 1-3
  • Introductory kata

Yellow to Orange

  • Naihanchi structure and turns
  • Basic combinations
  • Front fall and roll introduction
  • Two standing locks
  • Targeting and first-aid rules

Orange to Green

  • Pinan / Heian kata set
  • Padwork combinations
  • Tuite lock flows
  • Fundamental off-balances
  • Modern Arnis 12 strikes

Green to Purple

  • Seisan introduction or equivalent set
  • Spiral control and two-hand rule
  • High fall preparation
  • Push and grab scenarios

Purple to Brown

  • Advanced kata per instructor
  • Hip and leg entries
  • Tapi-tapi sequences
  • Standing-to-ground transitions

Brown to Black

  • Kata review and bunkai presentation
  • Lead warm-up and drill
  • Controlled pressure test
  • Assist in at least 10 classes
